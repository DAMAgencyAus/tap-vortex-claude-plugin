# Privacy and consent basics for shopper memory

General information only, not legal advice. Rules change; check the official sources below and get a professional check for anything sensitive or unusual. Tell the user this whenever you summarise these points.

## Australia

### Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles (APPs)

- **Who it covers:** most businesses with annual turnover above $3 million, plus some smaller businesses (for example those that trade in personal information or provide health services). Many small online stores may be exempt, but the exemption has conditions and has been under review, so treat the APPs as good practice anyway. Shoppers expect it, and email and payment platforms often require it.
- **Be open (APP 1):** have a clear privacy policy that says what you collect, why, how it's used and shared, and how to contact you.
- **Collect only what's reasonably necessary (APP 3).** Sensitive information, which includes health information, generally needs the person's consent to collect.
- **Tell people at the time of collection (APP 5):** a short note next to the question or sign-up ("We'll use this to suggest products and remember your choice next time").
- **Use it for the reason you collected it (APP 6),** or a related reason the person would reasonably expect.
- **Direct marketing (APP 7):** give an easy way to opt out, and honour it.
- **Overseas disclosure (APP 8):** many tools (email platforms, analytics) store data overseas. Say so in the privacy policy.
- **Keep it secure (APP 11),** and delete or de-identify it when no longer needed.
- **Access and correction (APPs 12 and 13):** shoppers can ask to see and correct what you hold.
- **Notifiable Data Breaches scheme:** businesses covered by the Act must notify affected people and the OAIC of eligible data breaches.

Source: Office of the Australian Information Commissioner, https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles

### Spam Act 2003 (email and SMS marketing)

- **Consent:** send commercial emails or SMS only with consent (express, such as a ticked sign-up, or in limited cases inferred).
- **Identify:** say who the message is from, with contact details.
- **Unsubscribe:** every message needs a working unsubscribe, actioned within five working days.

Source: Australian Communications and Media Authority, https://www.acma.gov.au/avoid-sending-spam

### Cookies and tracking

Australia has no cookie-banner rule like the EU's, but the APPs still apply to personal information collected through tracking. Explain tracking in the privacy policy.

## EU and UK (GDPR, UK GDPR, ePrivacy / PECR)

Applies when the store targets or monitors shoppers in the EU or UK, wherever the store is based.

- **Lawful basis:** each use needs one (often consent for marketing and non-essential tracking; contract for processing an order; legitimate interests for some personalisation, with a balancing test).
- **Cookie consent:** non-essential cookies and similar tracking (analytics, ad pixels, most personalisation that stores identifiers) need opt-in consent before they run. Essential cookies (cart, checkout) don't.
- **Data minimisation and purpose limitation:** collect only what's needed for a stated purpose.
- **Special category data,** including health, needs explicit consent or another specific condition.
- **Rights:** access, correction, deletion, objection (including to direct marketing at any time), and portability.
- **Processors:** have a data processing agreement with tools that hold shopper data (email, analytics, app providers).
- **International transfers:** data leaving the EU/UK needs a transfer mechanism (adequacy decision or standard contractual clauses).
- **Retention:** set and state how long each kind of data is kept.

Sources: European Data Protection Board, https://www.edpb.europa.eu ; UK Information Commissioner's Office, https://ico.org.uk

## Practical checklist for a vortex

1. Each remembered item has a reason tied to a later step that helps the shopper.
2. A short note where answers are collected, linking to the privacy policy.
3. Marketing consent is separate from answering questions. Answering a question is not consent to email.
4. Guesses are labelled as suggestions and easy to change.
5. Health or other sensitive answers: ask for consent plainly, use only for matching, and get a professional check.
6. Shoppers can ask to see or delete their data, and the store can actually do it.
7. EU/UK shoppers: tracking and personalisation that rely on non-essential cookies wait for consent.
8. Set a retention period (for example, anonymous browsing memory expires after a set time).
9. The store owns its data; don't sell it or use it to train outside models without clear permission.
