# What to look for in each leak area

Use these prompts while inspecting a store. They are questions to ask, not a checklist to recite. Report only what applies to this store.

## 1. Arrival and ad match

- If the user shared ads: does the landing page repeat the ad's problem, words and picture within the first screen?
- Do all ads go to the homepage, even when each ad makes a different promise?
- Is there a popup, cookie banner or announcement bar that covers the first screen on a phone?
- Would a stranger know within a few seconds that they're in the right place?

## 2. Offer clarity

- Headline: does it say what the product does for the shopper, or is it a brand slogan?
- Is there one clear next step, or several competing buttons?
- Is the current offer (discount, gift, free shipping, bundle price) stated plainly, with any conditions?
- Is the price visible without hunting?

## 3. Product-finding

- How many products are in the range? Is there help choosing (by need, skin type, size, use, experience level), or only a grid?
- Can filters or collections be understood by someone who doesn't know the brand's product names?
- Do product names explain what they are, or are they internal names ("The Aurora")?
- For a shopper who isn't sure, is there any path that says "this one is for you, and here's why"?
- Variants: is it clear which size, shade or strength to choose?

## 4. Proof and education

- Reviews: are there any, are they near the add-to-cart button, do they mention the specific problem shoppers have?
- Is the main doubt for this kind of product answered? (Will it work for me? Will it fit? Is it safe? Is it worth the price? What if I don't like it?)
- Guarantees, returns, delivery times: visible on the product page, or buried in the footer?
- Is there a short "how it works" or "how to use" for products that need it?
- Are claims backed by something (photos, numbers the store can support, certifications)? Flag claims that look unsupported, gently.

## 5. Bag and order size

- Is there a natural second item (refill, accessory, set, bigger size)? Is it offered at the moment it makes sense?
- Is there a free-shipping threshold, and does the store show how close the shopper is?
- Do packs or bundles exist, and do they explain why they suit this shopper (not just "save 10%")?
- Does anything feel pushy or confusing (too many upsells, popups stacking up)?

## 6. Checkout path

- Steps between "I want this" and paying: cart page, drawer, extra upsell screens?
- Surprise costs: shipping or fees only shown at the end?
- Express payment options visible (Shop Pay, Apple Pay, Google Pay, PayPal, Afterpay)?
- Does the cart send shoppers away from the store to a different-looking site?
- Note: don't enter checkout. Report only what is visible before it.

## 7. Follow-up

- Is there a sign-up form with a real reason to join, beyond "10% off"?
- If the store's emails are visible (user-provided), do they reflect what the shopper chose or looked at?
- Is there likely an abandoned-cart or browse-abandonment follow-up? (Ask; don't assume.)

## 8. Return memory

- Does anything on the site suggest it remembers a returning visitor: recently viewed, "welcome back", saved bag, account perks?
- If a shopper was told "this one fits you" (by a quiz, chat, staff or email), would they see the same match next visit, or have to start again?
- Do email links drop people on the homepage, or on the exact product and offer the email was about?

## 9. Refills and win-back

- For consumables: is there a subscription, refill reminder or "running low?" offer?
- For durables: is there a reason to come back (care products, accessories, upgrades, gifts)?
- Is there any sign of a win-back message for people who bought once and stopped?

## 10. Mobile (inferred)

- Viewport tag present?
- How far down the page is the first add-to-cart or main button likely to sit on a phone?
- Large hero images or videos before any useful text?
- Tiny links or buttons, or text over busy images?
- Popups or sticky bars that would cover much of a small screen?
- Remember: say these are inferred from the page code, not measured on a device.
