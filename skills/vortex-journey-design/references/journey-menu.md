# The vortex journey menu

Every step below is optional. Pick the steps that help this store's shoppers, in the order that suits them. Nothing here is a template, and no step has a fixed length, number of questions or discount. A vortex can replace one page, the homepage, or the whole store (site-wide).

For each step: what it's for, what it can use from memory, and what it can add.

## Arrive (the ad or link they came from)

- **For:** carrying the exact promise of the ad, email or link through to the page, so the shopper knows instantly they're in the right place (scent).
- **Uses:** which ad, angle, campaign or email brought them.
- **Adds:** entry angle, source.
- **Ideas:** a different opening per ad angle; same words, same problem, same visual style as the ad. See the `vortex-ad-scent` skill.

## Offer

- **For:** a clear reason to keep going, shaped to what the store can afford.
- **Uses:** entry angle, returning-shopper status.
- **Adds:** which offer they saw.
- **Ideas:** a gift that builds with the bag, free shipping at a threshold that fits the margins, a starter pack, a first-order perk. Never assume a discount; ask what the margins allow.

## Optional quick question or two

- **For:** only when the range is big enough or the choice confusing enough that a question genuinely helps.
- **Uses:** entry angle (skip questions the ad already answered).
- **Adds:** what they told you (problem, goal, size, experience, preference).
- **Ideas:** a single tap on "what brings you here?", a path choice, or none at all. Keep it short; never make it the centre of the vortex.

## Education and proof

- **For:** answering the doubt that stops this shopper, at the moment they feel it.
- **Uses:** their problem or goal, the product they're looking at.
- **Adds:** which proof they viewed.
- **Ideas:** reviews from people with the same problem, a short "how it works", before-and-after (only if real), delivery and returns promises, a guarantee.

## Their match, and why it fits

- **For:** showing the right product for this person, and the reason in their words.
- **Uses:** everything told and done so far.
- **Adds:** the match, alternatives they compared.
- **Ideas:** one main match with a "why it suits you" line, a couple of alternatives, easy to change. Matching can learn over time: the system suggests, the store owner approves.

## Packs, bundles and a bag that builds toward their offer

- **For:** a bigger order that makes sense for the shopper, not a pushy upsell.
- **Uses:** match, problem, offer.
- **Adds:** bag contents, packs viewed, add-ons accepted or skipped.
- **Ideas:** a pack sized to how they'll use it, a bag that shows how close they are to the gift or free shipping, a sensible second item. Always skippable.

## Product popups

- **For:** letting shoppers explore any product without losing their place.
- **Uses:** their answers, to add a "why it suits you" line.
- **Adds:** products viewed.
- **Ideas:** a popup that explains the product like a good product page (benefits, details, reviews), not an upsell.

## Checkout

- **For:** going straight to checkout, all inside their store, with nothing lost.
- **Uses:** bag, offer.
- **Adds:** the order, and the reason behind it (which match, which ad, which answers), stored on the order.

## Follow-ups that know what they chose (email/SMS, often Klaviyo)

- **For:** messages that continue the conversation instead of a generic newsletter.
- **Uses:** match, bag, viewed products, where they stopped.
- **Adds:** opens, clicks, replies.
- **Ideas:** "still deciding between X and Y?" for those who almost bought; "how to get the best from X" after purchase; links that reopen their exact match and bag.

## Next visit

- **For:** never starting from scratch.
- **Uses:** everything remembered.
- **Adds:** return visit.
- **Ideas:** "welcome back", the same match, the same bag, recently viewed.

## Refills and replenishment

- **For:** consumables that run out.
- **Uses:** order, pack size, typical usage the store knows.
- **Adds:** reorder or swap.
- **Ideas:** a reminder timed to when they'll run low, a one-tap reorder of the same thing, an easy swap.

## Win-back

- **For:** shoppers who bought once and drifted.
- **Uses:** what they bought and why.
- **Adds:** response to win-back.
- **Ideas:** something genuinely new for their need, not just a bigger discount.

## Post-purchase

- **For:** turning a first order into a good experience and a second order.
- **Uses:** the order and the reason behind it.
- **Adds:** reviews, referrals, care questions.
- **Ideas:** setup or usage help, a review request that asks about their specific problem, a referral when they're happiest.

## What the store owner gets back

Every step above also feeds the owner's view:

- **Shopper stories:** the journey of each shopper, even anonymous ones.
- **What shoppers are telling you:** demand, in their words (problems, goals, requests).
- **Who almost bought:** where they stopped and what was in their bag.
- **Which ad brings which shopper:** entry angle against match and order.
- **Matching that learns:** suggestions the owner approves.
- **A Monday briefing:** what changed last week and what to do next.
- **The reason on every order.**
- **Audiences from answers:** for ads and email.
- **The store owns all its data.**
